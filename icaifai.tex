\begin{abstract}
University campus networks combine unmanaged BYOD endpoints, open research laboratories, and high-density IoT deployments, creating an expansive attack surface where traditional Security Operations Center (SOC) workflows struggle with long operational Mean Time to Respond (MTTR). We propose an AI-native, event-driven edge--cloud security platform engineered specifically for autonomous campus infrastructure defense. The architecture deploys lightweight INT8-quantized TCN--GRU sequence models at network edge gateways to score non-DPI flow telemetry. Enriched security events are carried asynchronously over a Redis Streams consumer group to a cloud risk resolver and Deep Q-Network (DQN) Software-Defined Networking (SDN) policy that emits SOAR playbooks; installing them in the dataplane is outside what we measure. Evaluated on \textbf{real InSDN} flow windows~\cite{b1} ($20{,}000$ stratified samples), the platform achieves a \emph{software control-path} latency --- edge inference, identity enrichment, Redis \texttt{XADD}/\texttt{XREADGROUP}, DQN selection, and playbook construction, excluding dataplane enforcement --- with a $4.194$~ms median and a $7.470$~ms p99 on localhost Redis~7.2.4. Because stride-1 windows overlap by nine of ten timesteps, we evaluate under a temporally blocked and purged split with a train-only scaler; comparing that protocol to a conventional random split yields a near-null leakage effect ($|\Delta F1| < 0.002$ at fixed $\tau$). Under the controlled protocol the INT8 detector reaches $F1 = 0.9340$ at $\tau = 0.65$ with a $0.048$~MB footprint and negligible quantization loss ($|\Delta F1| \le 0.0002$). We further report five negative results: gradient-boosted trees on the same features outperform the proposed detector (LightGBM $F1 = 0.9886$ against $0.9723$), leaving it only a footprint advantage; reconstruction-error scoring is unusable on this attack-heavy corpus ($F1 \approx 0.035$), so detection rests entirely on the supervised head; our edge CPU budget is missed ($36.0\%$ of one logical CPU at a sustained $10{,}000$ windows/s against a pre-specified $10\%$, with \texttt{torch.set\_num\_threads(1)}); zero-shot transfer to CIC-IDS2017 collapses ($F1 = 0.456$); and localhost Redis Streams is not faster than a tight REST loopback, so the bus is justified by the multi-consumer deployment model rather than single-host IPC speed. We provide an ablation study, policy benchmarks against a brute-force oracle, and a deterministic reproducibility artifact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is reshaping higher-education digital infrastructure, but university campus cybersecurity faces an increasingly intractable threat landscape: IoT sensors, student Bring-Your-Own-Device (BYOD) endpoints, open research laboratories, and administrative databases share one network fabric. Centralized SIEM and human-in-the-loop SOC pipelines exhibit long MTTR during volumetric DDoS, ransomware lateral movement, or data exfiltration~\cite{b4}, causing analyst burnout and delayed containment.

To bridge this gap we propose an \textbf{AI-Native Event-Driven Edge--Cloud Security Architecture}. Rather than bolting AI onto legacy SOC dashboards, our design embeds it in the control loop: lightweight sequence detectors sit at edge gateways while an event-driven bus streams identity-enriched telemetry to a cloud Deep Q-Network (DQN) controller for SDN/SOAR actuation.

\subsection{Research Questions}
This investigation is guided by four formal Research Questions (RQs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Control-Path Latency):} Can an event-driven edge--cloud architecture reduce the software decision path, from telemetry ingestion to containment playbook emission, to sub-10~ms levels?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Quantization \& Accuracy):} Does INT8 dynamic quantization maintain NIDS classification accuracy while satisfying an edge gateway \emph{model-footprint} constraint ($\le 1$~MB of model storage)?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Reinforcement Learning Efficiency):} How closely can a DQN approximate the reward-optimal action under a modeled single-step contention environment relative to static and heuristic policies?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Domain Shift Generalization):} How effectively does an in-domain trained edge model generalize zero-shot across disparate network datasets (InSDN $\rightarrow$ CIC-IDS2017)?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Formal Hypotheses}
We test two explicit hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Quantization Degradation):} INT8 dynamic quantization incurs less than $0.5\%$ $F1$-score degradation ($|\Delta F1| \le 0.005$) compared to full-precision FP32 models.
    \item \textbf{H2 (Edge Resource Feasibility):} Edge inference requires $\le 10\%$ of one logical CPU (\texttt{psutil} convention: $100\% =$ one core fully busy) under a sustained offered load of $10{,}000$~telemetry windows/sec with a single PyTorch host thread.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}
The principal contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled AI-Native Pipeline:} An event-driven architecture combining PyTorch INT8 TCN--GRU edge detectors, identity context fusion, and a Redis Streams consumer-group event transport, with a DQN policy that \emph{emits} OpenFlow playbooks (rate limiting, rerouting, isolation, token revocation). We measure the software path that produces those playbooks, not their installation in the dataplane.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Validation \& Negative Results:} Microbenchmarks on real InSDN~\cite{b1} traffic, with Redis Streams in the timed path, ablation, policy benchmarks against a brute-force oracle, saturation analysis, and a CIC-IDS2017~\cite{b2} holdout. We report five negative results --- failure of reconstruction-error scoring, a near-null leakage effect from stride-1 overlap, rejection of our edge CPU budget (H2), severe zero-shot transfer failure, and gradient-boosted trees outperforming the proposed detector --- that bound where this design is deployable.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Artifact:} A reproducibility artifact regenerating every table from one seeded entry point, at \url{https://github.com/thtcsec/icai-fai2026}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
SDN intrusion datasets such as InSDN~\cite{b1} and CIC-IDS2017~\cite{b2} have established standard benchmarks for network intrusion detection. Sequence models such as Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN)~\cite{b3} employ causal dilated convolutions to capture long-range temporal dependencies with parallelizable training compared to recurrent LSTM architectures. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has been applied to DDoS defense and 6G control loops~\cite{b4}. Closing the loop is not itself novel: Liu \emph{et al.}~\cite{b27} mitigate SDN flooding online with DRL, and Yungaicela-Naula \emph{et al.}~\cite{b28} integrate a DL detector with a DRL mitigator on Mininet and ONOS and evaluate autonomous response against live traffic. We therefore make no priority claim over autonomous mitigation. What is less well covered is the composition: an event-driven edge--cloud split in which quantized detectors run on the gateway and policy runs in the cloud, with the software path between them quantified end to end, and with leakage control, quantization cost, domain shift and negative results reported rather than elided.

Compressing detectors for constrained gateways is an active line: pruned and quantized LSTM ensembles on Raspberry~Pi~\cite{b20} and INT8 TensorFlow Lite CNNs on ARM Cortex-M~\cite{b21} both find INT8 accuracy-neutral at far lower footprint, consistent with our H1, but both stop at the detector and close no loop onto actuation. Conversely, surveys of agent-driven security operations~\cite{b22} catalogue triage automation while noting that end-to-end autonomous remediation remains prospective; our work sits between the two. A parallel thread concerns evaluation rigour: Arp \emph{et al.}~\cite{b23} show data snooping is endemic in learning-based security papers and inflates results, and leakage-free reassessments of ML-NIDS confirm preprocessing alone moves headline metrics~\cite{b24}. We adopt those recommendations in Section~\ref{sec:protocol} and report their cost in Section~\ref{sec:leakage_effect}.

\section{AI-Native System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
The architecture comprises four decoupled tiers: Edge Telemetry Taps, Identity Context Fusion, Redis Stream Event Bus, and Cloud DQN Control.

\subsection{Edge Telemetry and TCN--GRU Detector}
Edge taps capture flow headers and extract 10-D flow feature vectors over sliding time windows ($\Delta t = 1.0$~s). A hybrid TCN--GRU network processes the feature sequences, outputting both a reconstruction $\hat{X}$ with error $RE(x) = \|X - \hat{X}\|_2^2$ and classification logits.

\textbf{Training objective.} The model is trained \emph{multi-task}, which determines how the results should be read. Each epoch runs a reconstruction pass minimizing $\mathrm{MSE}(X,\hat{X})$ over \emph{normal windows only}, then a joint pass minimizing $\mathrm{MSE}(X,\hat{X}) + \lambda\,\mathrm{CE}(\text{logits}, y)$ over all windows with $\lambda = 0.5$; since InSDN is attack-heavy, the cross-entropy up-weights the normal class by the attack-to-normal ratio. The detector is therefore \emph{not} unsupervised: it requires labels, and the autoencoder branch is an auxiliary representation objective, not the decision rule. Detection evaluates the supervised head, $P(\text{attack}) = 1 - P(\text{normal})$, against $\tau$; reconstruction error is not the operational score, for the reason in Section~\ref{sec:re_vs_cls}.

\subsection{Dynamic INT8 Quantization}
To satisfy memory-constrained edge gateways, PyTorch~\cite{b12} dynamic INT8 quantization compresses linear and GRU layer weight matrices from 32-bit floating point to 8-bit integers. We report no feature attribution study, since our artifact does not implement one and we prefer to leave the question open rather than assert an unmeasured importance ranking. Identifying which of the 10 features carry the discriminative signal, and whether that ranking is what fails to transfer (Section~\ref{sec:cross}), is future work.

\subsection{Identity Context Fusion \& Privacy Preservation}
Edge security events are enriched by querying local DHCP/RADIUS tables to append spatial-temporal context vectors:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E} = \{ \text{FlowID}, \text{RE}(x), P(\text{attack}), \text{UserRole}, \text{TrustScore} \}.
\end{equation}
To support GDPR data-minimization principles, raw payload inspection (DPI) is omitted entirely; only header-level statistical metadata is processed, and user identifiers are pseudonymized prior to cloud transport.

\subsection{Cloud Risk Engine and DQN SDN Control}
Enriched events stream asynchronously to Redis topic \texttt{security:telemetry:stream}. The cloud risk engine calculates a composite risk index $R$ over the detector's attack probability, the identity trust score, and asset criticality:
\begin{equation}
R = w_1 P(\text{attack}) + w_2 (1-\text{TrustScore}) + w_3 \text{AssetCriticality},
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
with $(w_1, w_2, w_3) = (0.60, 0.25, 0.15)$ in all reported experiments; the fused term is the classifier probability, not $RE(x)$. When identity is ablated the trust term is undefined and its weight folds into detection, giving $R = 0.85\,P(\text{attack}) + 0.15\,\text{AssetCriticality}$, keeping scores comparable across arms. The escalation ladder maps $R \ge 0.70$ to critical (block and revoke token), $R \ge 0.50$ to high (quarantine VLAN), and $R \ge 0.35$ to medium (throttle bandwidth).

Two caveats. The weights are hand-set, not learned, and encode a design prior we do not claim is optimal. More importantly, InSDN carries no asset inventory, so AssetCriticality is drawn i.i.d.\ uniform on $[0,1]$ in every experiment and is pure noise; the third term therefore \emph{degrades} our numbers, and the risk-engine benefit reported later comes from the trust term alone.

At the selected severity, the Deep Q-Network (DQN) policy agent~\cite{b5} evaluates state vector $s_t$ and selects containment action $a_t \in \{a_0: \text{Forward}, a_1: \text{Rate-Limit}, a_2: \text{Reroute}, a_3: \text{Isolate}\}$ to actuate automated SOAR playbooks~\cite{b18}. Compared to static rule tables, DQN adjusts policy choices across contention states without manual threshold maintenance.

\subsection{Dataset Sampling \& Leakage Control}
\label{sec:protocol}
We evaluate on the public \textbf{InSDN} dataset~\cite{b1}. Sliding-window aggregation of the released flow records ($\Delta t = 1.0$~s, sequence length $T=10$, \emph{stride 1}) yields $49{,}991$ windows, of which $40{,}043$ are attack and $9{,}948$ normal. Note that $49{,}991$ is the size of \emph{our} windowed corpus, not InSDN's native flow count.

Window $i$ shares $T-1 = 9$ of its $10$ timesteps with each neighbour, so an i.i.d.\ random split places near-duplicate observations on both sides of the train/test boundary --- precisely the sampling and snooping pitfalls catalogued in~\cite{b23,b24}. Our protocol instead (i) cuts the temporally ordered corpus into $20$ contiguous blocks and assigns whole blocks to train or test; (ii) \emph{purges} $9$ windows from both ends of every block, guaranteeing no surviving training window shares even a single timestep with a test window; (iii) stratified-subsamples each side independently to $14{,}000$ train and $6{,}000$ test windows, preserving class balance; and (iv) fits the scaler on the training partition only, transforming the test partition and the CIC-IDS2017 holdout with those frozen statistics. We verified that zero test windows are byte-identical to any training window. Section~\ref{sec:leakage_effect} quantifies what this protocol is actually worth.

\subsection{Why Reconstruction Error Is Not the Decision Rule}
\label{sec:re_vs_cls}
Since much of the sequence-autoencoder IDS literature scores on reconstruction error alone, it is worth reporting which of our two outputs actually works. We evaluated the standard unsupervised rule $\tau = \mu + k\sigma$, with $\mu, \sigma$ estimated on reconstruction errors of \emph{normal training} windows, for $k \in \{0.5, \ldots, 3.0\}$. It fails comprehensively: the best setting $k=0.5$ yields $F1 = 0.0354$; detection performance is thus entirely attributable to the supervised head, and the architecture should be cited as a supervised sequence classifier with an auxiliary reconstruction objective. The classifier head defaults to six logits for checkpoint compatibility, but training collapses labels to binary normal-vs-attack and scores $1-P(\text{normal})$, so classes $2$--$5$ are unsupervised in the reported experiments.

\subsection{Throughput Saturation \& Edge Feasibility (H2)}
Per-inference latency and throughput reconcile only once the batching regime is stated, so we report both, each as the median of $5$ repeats. Run flat out with a single PyTorch host thread, the detector saturates at $662$~windows/s unbatched, rising through $10{,}302$ at batch $=32$ to $51{,}239$ at batch $=512$: batching, not the model, is what reaches campus scale. Saturation remains the least stable quantity we measure, so we rest no claim on it. Table~\ref{tab:resource} instead reports the \emph{paced} offered load at batch $=128$.

\textbf{CPU metric.} We report \texttt{psutil.Process.cpu\_percent()} over the paced wall-clock window with \emph{no} division by \texttt{cpu\_count()}: $100\%$ means one logical CPU fully busy. Dividing by logical CPU count would convert the reading into a fraction of total machine capacity and must not be labelled ``\% of one core.'' PyTorch is pinned with \texttt{torch.set\_num\_threads(1)} so the unit matches H2. At low offered rates the window is sleep-dominated, so the reading understates busy-period cost; the H2 decision uses the $10{,}000$~windows/s row.

\textbf{H2 is rejected.} The hypothesis asserted $\le 10\%$ of one logical CPU at a sustained $10{,}000$~windows/s. The system sustains that load ($9{,}998$ of $10{,}000$ offered windows/s in every repeat) while consuming a median $36.0\%$ of one logical CPU (range $35.0$--$49.1\%$ across repeats), about $3.6\times$ the budget. Memory is favourable: peak working set is flat at $\approx 541$~MB, dominated by the Python/PyTorch runtime rather than the $0.048$~MB model. The system is memory- but not CPU-feasible on one commodity logical CPU under this runtime; a compiled ONNX or TensorRT path is the obvious next step.

We deliberately omit a legacy inline-DPI comparison. We have no DPI implementation on this testbed, and a baseline synthesised from an analytic formula would not constitute an experimental result.

\subsection{Detector Baseline Comparison \& Quantization Degradation (H1)}
\label{sec:sota}
Table~\ref{tab:sota} compares our INT8 TCN--GRU against unsupervised, tabular and sequence baselines trained on the identical InSDN split; Transformer AE is a 2-layer encoder-decoder with 4 attention heads and masked reconstruction loss. Every model is scored at its own optimal threshold, since a threshold favouring one architecture need not suit another. \emph{That threshold is selected by searching the test split}, so each $F1$ here is an optimistic bound rather than a deployable operating point; the identical search is applied to every model, so comparison between rows stays internally fair. The tree ensembles receive the window flattened to $T \times d = 100$ features --- the same raw information the sequence models see --- so the comparison does not handicap them.

\textbf{Gradient-boosted trees beat the proposed detector, and this is a negative result.} LightGBM~\cite{b25} reaches $F1 = 0.9886$ and XGBoost~\cite{b26} $0.9880$ against $0.9723$ for INT8 TCN--GRU, and XGBoost does it at $0.399$~ms per inference against $1.935$~ms. Random Forest also exceeds TCN--GRU ($0.9843$). On ten aggregate flow statistics, the temporal structure a TCN--GRU exists to exploit is evidently not where the discriminative signal lives. One advantage survives: the INT8 model footprint is $0.048$~MB against serialized tree objects of $0.845$--$20.6$~MB, but the RQ2 budget was $1$~MB and XGBoost fits inside it.

The Isolation Forest row uses \texttt{contamination="auto"} on time-averaged windows; on an $80\%$-attack corpus this heuristic is badly mismatched, so $F1 = 0.2000$ evidences that an untuned classical outlier detector fails at this class balance rather than serving as a tuned baseline.

INT8 quantization reduces the model footprint from $0.104$~MB to $0.048$~MB at negligible accuracy cost ($|\Delta F1| = 0.0002$), supporting hypothesis \textbf{H1}. Since the RQ2 budget was $1$~MB and the FP32 model already meets it at $0.104$~MB, the honest reading of H1 is that quantization is \emph{harmless} rather than necessary for gateways at this memory scale.

\subsection{Component-Wise Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} details the impact of removing components. Because each configuration scores on a different scale, we select the threshold on the training split and apply it unchanged to test; a shared fixed threshold would measure calibration mismatch instead of component contribution. InSDN has no identity annotations, so trust comes from a simulated overlay parameterised by $\rho = P(\text{low trust} \mid \text{attack})$, reported at $\rho = 0.7$.

The result contradicts our expectation. Identity fusion and the risk engine \emph{do not} improve $F1$; removing them nudges it from $0.9616$ up to $0.9680$. What they buy is false-positive suppression: FPR falls from $23.77\%$ under direct edge actuation to $16.74\%$. Since the controller autonomously isolates flows, trading a fraction of a point of $F1$ for fewer false alarms is operationally correct --- but it is a trade, not a free gain. A sweep over $\rho \in [0.5, 0.9]$ (in the artifact) shows identity fusion improves $F1$ only once the overlay is strongly informative ($\rho \ge 0.8$).

\textbf{Redis vs.\ REST on loopback.} Transport costs are measured, not assumed: Redis Streams \texttt{XADD}+\texttt{XREADGROUP} averages $1.293$~ms per event, while a synchronous localhost HTTP POST averages $0.799$~ms. Consequently the ``w/o Redis'' arm is \emph{faster} on this single-host harness. We retain Redis because the architecture targets an asynchronous multi-consumer cloud path, not because it wins a localhost IPC microbenchmark.

\subsection{Policy Control Benchmarks (RQ3)}
Table~\ref{tab:policy_benchmark} benchmarks the cloud DQN controller against static and heuristic baselines on a single-step contention environment whose reward $r(s,a)$ trades containment efficacy against collateral QoS damage and congestion relief. Because $r$ is analytic, the optimal action $a^*$ is obtained by exact enumeration over the four actions, giving a ground-truth oracle. The agent is trained by fitted Q-iteration over $4{,}000$ environment interactions with $\epsilon$-greedy exploration and evaluated on $5{,}000$ held-out states.

The DQN selects the oracle-optimal action in $98.8\%$ of states and attains essentially zero mean regret, against $38.2\%$ for a static confidence ladder and $34.8\%$ for a hand-tuned heuristic table. Two caveats temper this. First, the agent is trained on the same reward function the oracle maximises, so this shows function-approximation capacity within a known environment, not generalisation to a live campus. Second, the DQN inflicts \emph{more} raw QoS disruption on benign traffic ($0.128$) than the static ladder ($0.045$): reward-optimal behaviour is more aggressive than a conservative threshold rule.

\subsection{How Much Did Leakage Inflate the Numbers?}
\label{sec:leakage_effect}
Rather than assert our leakage controls were necessary, we measured what they are worth, rerunning the identical detector, seed, and schedule while changing only the split and scaler. Protocol~A is the unsound one (i.i.d.\ split over overlapping windows, scaler fit on the pooled corpus); Protocol~B is that of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}.

\textbf{The effect is near-null, and this is a negative result relative to our prior expectation.} On this run, Protocol~A yields fixed-threshold $F1 = 0.9322$ while Protocol~B yields $0.9340$ ($\Delta = -0.0018$); optimal-threshold $F1$ differs by only $+0.0014$. We therefore do not claim that blocked-and-purged splitting rescued a large inflated headline number here. Two explanations fit and we cannot separate them: overlapping windows may be less informative than they look, or this corpus may be easy enough that both protocols saturate above $0.93$. Blocked-and-purged splitting remains cheap and principled and we still recommend it; a reviewer should not assume a random split has by itself inflated a result by a large margin on this dataset.

\section{Threats to Validity \& Limitations}
\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:validity}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Internal Validity (measurement stability):} Timing on this host is noisy enough that single measurements are not results. Every timing figure is now a median over repeats --- $5$ for throughput and per-model latency, $1{,}000$ trials summarised by percentiles for the control path --- after we found that a single-run measurement had produced a throughput shortfall that repetition did not reproduce. Saturation throughput remains unstable between runs by more than a factor of two and we rest no claim on it. Accuracy metrics, by contrast, were bit-identical across all runs. Absolute latencies should be read as order-of-magnitude on this hardware, not as hardware-independent guarantees.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility:} An earlier version of this artifact constructed models before seeding the global RNG, so detection metrics varied between runs of the same script. \texttt{set\_global\_seed} now runs at the head of every generator, before any module is built, making each script bit-deterministic. It is \emph{not} re-invoked before each individual model, so a model built after several others inherits an RNG advanced by their construction --- which is exactly why the same detector scores $0.9679$ in Section~\ref{sec:threshold} and $0.9723$ in Section~\ref{sec:sota}. Determinism at a fixed seed is not robustness across seeds; accuracy is single-seed throughout.
    \item \textbf{External Validity:} Evaluation uses public InSDN and CIC benchmarks; broader IoT/IIoT corpora such as UNSW-NB15~\cite{b6}, ToN\_IoT~\cite{b7}, and Edge-IIoTset~\cite{b8} offer complementary vectors for multi-campus validation.
    \item \textbf{Construct Validity:} Metrics assume clean flow labels; noisy headers could affect precision. We report no human-SOC comparison: we have no measured analyst baseline, and a stipulated one would establish only that milliseconds beat minutes, which needs no experiment.
    \item \textbf{Residual Leakage Risk:} Purging guarantees no shared timestep, but blocks are cut over the released record ordering, a proxy for session boundaries rather than ground-truth grouping, so a single long attack episode could straddle two blocks. Session-aware grouping would be stronger; InSDN does not expose the required metadata.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations \& Future Work}
Primary limitations: (i) a single-controller emulated SDN testbed rather than production hardware; (ii) no evaluation of adversarial robustness against evasion; (iii) severe zero-shot cross-dataset degradation; (iv) rejection of H2 on its CPU term, costing a median $36.0\%$ of one logical CPU against a $10\%$ budget at $10{,}000$~windows/s; (v) a detector outperformed on these features by off-the-shelf gradient boosting, leaving its case resting on footprint alone; (vi) Redis Streams measured on localhost, so cross-host network RTT is uncounted, and localhost REST can beat Redis on IPC alone; (vii) single-seed reporting of accuracy; and (viii) identity context remains a simulated overlay. Future work: domain adaptation, compiled ONNX/TensorRT edge runtimes, online threshold tuning, multi-seed reporting, and federated multi-campus learning.

\section{Ethics, Privacy \& Public Artifact}
\subsection{Privacy Posture}
The architecture operates strictly on non-DPI flow headers; payloads are uninspected and IP/MAC identifiers are \emph{pseudonymized} via SHA-256 before transport, supporting data minimization under GDPR and Vietnam's Decree 13/2023. We avoid ``anonymized'': an unkeyed SHA-256 over a $32$-bit IPv4 or $48$-bit MAC address is trivially invertible by enumeration. A keyed HMAC with a rotated per-deployment secret is the defensible construction; the unkeyed hash is a prototype placeholder, not a privacy guarantee.

\subsection{Public Research Artifact}
Code, preprocessors, checkpoints, Docker files, and scripts are available under MIT License at \url{https://github.com/thtcsec/icai-fai2026}. Every table is regenerated by one entry point, \texttt{results/run\_all\_and\_summarize.py}, which seeds all generators (seed \texttt{42}) before model construction and emits the machine-readable summary the tables are transcribed from.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented an AI-native event-driven edge--cloud architecture for autonomous campus network security. Combining INT8-quantized TCN--GRU edge detectors, Redis Streams transport (\texttt{XADD}/\texttt{XREADGROUP}), and cloud DQN SDN actuation, it achieves a software control path with a $4.194$~ms median and a $7.470$~ms p99 on localhost Redis~7.2.4, from edge inference to playbook construction. We stop short of calling this closed-loop mitigation: dataplane enforcement is unmeasured and the SOC comparison uses stipulated baselines.

The results we think transfer beyond this system are the negative ones. Gradient-boosted trees on the same features beat the proposed detector ($F1 = 0.9886$ for LightGBM against $0.9723$). Reconstruction-error scoring is unusable on an attack-heavy corpus ($F1 \approx 0.035$). Leakage from stride-1 overlap is near-null on this run ($|\Delta F1| < 0.002$). H2 fails on its CPU term, sustaining $10{,}000$ windows/s but at $36.0\%$ of one logical CPU against a $10\%$ budget. Zero-shot transfer to CIC-IDS2017 collapses to $F1 = 0.456$. Together these bound where an AI-native campus defense is currently practical, and they point the architecture's value at integration and actuation rather than at the choice of detector.
