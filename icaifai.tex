\begin{abstract}
Modern university campus networks present high device heterogeneity, open Wi-Fi topologies, and massive concurrent telemetry streams. Traditional Security Operations Center (SOC) architectures rely on centralized, manual incident response workflows that suffer from long Mean Time to Respond (MTTR $>$ 30 minutes) and severe alert fatigue. In this paper, we propose an \textbf{AI-Native Autonomous Security Platform} engineered for university environments. By decoupling detection into lightweight edge anomaly classifiers (Isolation Forests) and streaming high-confidence events over a Redis Stream bus to a cloud-based dynamic risk resolver and automated SOAR engine, our platform achieves sub-10ms event-to-mitigation latency. We present a fully reproducible prototype and evaluate it empirically across four benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate an end-to-end response latency of 4.2~ms, a 99.9\% reduction in MTTR compared to manual triage, an F1-score of 0.94 at optimal decision threshold ($\tau=0.65$), and minimal edge CPU overhead (14.3\% utilization at 10,000 events/sec).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a foundational technology driving digital transformation across higher education and industrial sectors. However, managing security within modern university environments introduces distinct architectural hurdles. High-density IoT sensors, unmanaged student BYOD endpoints, open research laboratories, and administrative databases co-exist on shared physical networks. Traditional firewall appliances and centralized SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) platforms lack the capability to perform zero-trust micro-segmentation in real time without introducing significant latency bottlenecks.

To address these challenges, we introduce an AI-native autonomous security framework. Our design contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lightweight AI-Native Architecture}: A decoupled edge-cloud event-driven pipeline featuring Isolation Forest anomaly detection at network taps and dynamic identity context fusion.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Incident Response Prototype}: An end-to-end prototype executing zero-trust mitigation playbooks (IP containment, credential revocation) without human-in-the-loop delays.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Evaluation Framework}: A reproducible benchmark suite quantifying latency, MTTR, detection sensitivity, and resource overhead under high throughput.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Architecture}
The proposed platform follows an event-driven edge-cloud paradigm. Edge nodes capture raw streaming telemetry vectors $X = [f_{\text{duration}}, f_{\text{pkt\_count}}, f_{\text{byte\_rate}}, f_{\text{syn\_ratio}}, f_{\text{port\_entropy}}]$ over sliding time windows $\Delta t = 1.0\text{~s}$. An unsupervised Isolation Forest model predicts anomaly scores $s(x, n)$. Telemetry events exceeding threshold $\tau_{\text{edge}} = 0.65$ trigger the Identity Fusion module, which enriches events with campus directory metadata (UserRole, DeviceType, TrustScore). Enriched events are published to a distributed Redis Stream event bus (\texttt{security:telemetry:stream}).

In the cloud control plane, a Dynamic Policy Engine computes a composite Risk Index $R$:
\begin{equation}
R = w_1 \cdot s(x, n) + w_2 \cdot (1 - \text{TrustScore}) + w_3 \cdot \text{AssetCriticality}
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
Events meeting $R \ge 0.70$ are classified as CRITICAL, instantly triggering automated SOAR playbooks for network isolation and token revocation.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
We evaluated our prototype using synthetic campus telemetry streams containing ground-truth anomaly annotations.

\subsection{Latency Breakdown (Contribution 1)}
We measured the execution latency across all four pipeline stages over 1,000 independent flow trials. The complete end-to-end pipeline achieves an average latency of $4.218\text{~ms}$, far below the sub-10ms operational target for real-time campus micro-segmentation.

\subsection{Mean Time to Respond (Contribution 2)}
The autonomous engine reduces response times from over 25 minutes (1,500s) to less than 10 milliseconds, representing a $>99.9\%$ efficiency gain.

At the optimal operational threshold $\tau = 0.65$, the system achieves Precision = 0.948, Recall = 0.932, F1-Score = 0.940, and a False Positive Rate (FPR) of 1.1\%.

\subsection{Edge Resource Scaling}
Under stress testing up to 10,000 events/sec, edge CPU utilization remained at 14.3\% with a RAM footprint of 49.0 MB, demonstrating feasibility for low-cost embedded gateway hardware.

\section{Conclusion \& Future Work}
We presented an AI-Native Autonomous Security Platform designed for higher education networks. Future work includes expanding the platform to multi-university federated learning consortiums and integrating local LLMs for automated root-cause reporting.
