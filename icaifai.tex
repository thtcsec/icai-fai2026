\begin{abstract}
University campus networks combine unmanaged BYOD endpoints, open laboratories, and high-density IoT, while traditional SOC workflows exhibit long operational Mean Time to Respond (MTTR). We present an AI-native, event-driven edge--cloud security platform tailored to campus infrastructure. Lightweight INT8-quantized TCN--GRU detectors at the edge publish enriched security events over Redis Streams to a cloud risk resolver and Deep Q-Network (DQN) Software-Defined Networking (SDN) controller that executes closed-loop SOAR playbooks. Evaluated on \textbf{real InSDN} flow windows (20{,}000 stratified samples from the public SDN intrusion corpus~\cite{b1}), the pipeline achieves end-to-end automated mitigation latency of $3.393 \pm 1.024$~ms (Mean~$\pm$~SD), reducing closed-loop containment time from $1518.5 \pm 335.3$~s (manual SOC triage) to $4.685 \pm 0.576$~ms, and attains F1~$=0.960 \pm 0.003$ at operating threshold $\tau=0.65$ (Precision~$=0.967$, Recall~$=0.953$, FPR~$=13.3\%$). The INT8 model footprint is $0.048$~MB with $6.3\%$ CPU/core under sustained 10{,}000~events/s load.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is reshaping higher-education digital infrastructure, yet campus cybersecurity remains difficult: IoT sensors, student BYOD devices, research laboratories, and administrative systems share the same underlay. Centralized SIEM/SOC pipelines still rely on human triage and often yield operational MTTR on the order of tens of minutes during DDoS, ransomware propagation, or exfiltration~\cite{b4}.

We propose an AI-native autonomous security architecture for university campuses. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A decoupled edge--cloud pipeline with INT8 TCN--GRU detection~\cite{b3}, identity fusion, and Redis Stream transport.
    \item A closed-loop DQN SDN controller with SOAR playbooks (Flowspec, 802.1X isolation, rerouting, token revocation) that outperforms rigid static lookup rules.
    \item A reproducible artifact trained and tested on \emph{real} InSDN windows~\cite{b1} (not synthetic-only), with measured latency/mitigation/detection/resource tables.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
SDN intrusion datasets such as InSDN~\cite{b1} and CSE-CIC-IDS2018/2017~\cite{b2} enabled reproducible NIDS evaluation beyond proprietary campus traces. Sequence models for temporal signals include Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN)~\cite{b3}, which offer parallelizable dilated convolutions compared with purely recurrent stacks. Reinforcement learning has been applied to networked DDoS defense and 6G control loops~\cite{b4}, but most prototypes stop at detection or offline policy learning rather than sub-10~ms closed-loop campus mitigation.

Compared with classical Isolation Forest baselines and heavier Transformer autoencoders, our design targets the campus constraint triad: (i)~edge memory, (ii)~deterministic event latency, and (iii)~autonomous containment without waiting for SOC queues. The gap we address is the missing end-to-end AI-native control path from edge telemetry to SDN/SOAR actuation under university threat models.

\subsection{Edge Telemetry and TCN--GRU Detector}
Edge taps vectorize flows into 10-D windows ($\Delta t=1$~s). A hybrid TCN--GRU emits reconstruction error $RE(x)$ and class logits. Primary detection uses $P(\mathrm{attack})=1-P(\mathrm{normal})$ with threshold $\tau$; $RE(x)$ still contributes to the cloud risk score. Adaptive unsupervised gating $\tau_{RE}=\mu_{RE}+k\sigma_{RE}$ is retained for risk fusion but is weaker alone on attack-heavy InSDN splits.

\subsection{INT8 Quantization, Identity Fusion, and Event Bus}
Dynamic INT8 quantization compresses Linear/GRU weights for edge gateways. Identity fusion attaches \{UserRole, DeviceType, TrustScore\} before publishing to Redis Stream \texttt{security:telemetry:stream}.

\subsection{Cloud Risk Engine and DQN SDN Control}
Composite risk is
\begin{equation}
R = w_1 RE(x) + w_2 (1-\mathrm{TrustScore}) + w_3 \mathrm{AssetCriticality}.
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
If $R\ge 0.70$, a DQN selects $a\in\{a_0,\ldots,a_3\}$ (forward, rate-limit, reroute, isolate) and SOAR playbooks actuate OpenFlow/Flowspec controls. Compared to static rule engines, DQN handles non-stationary multi-flow contention without manual threshold tuning, optimizing cumulative QoS while penalizing disruption.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented an AI-native campus security platform that closes the loop from edge TCN--GRU detection to DQN/SOAR actuation, validated on \textbf{real InSDN} traffic windows with $3.393 \pm 1.024$~ms end-to-end latency and $>99.99\%$ MTTR reduction versus manual SOC triage. Cross-dataset evaluation on CIC-IDS2017 exposes domain shift (F1 $0.961\rightarrow0.286$ at $\tau=0.65$), motivating future research on Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA), online continual threshold tuning, and federated multi-campus learning for autonomous zero-trust networks.
