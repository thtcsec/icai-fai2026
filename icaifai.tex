\begin{abstract}
University campus networks combine unmanaged BYOD endpoints, open research laboratories, and high-density IoT deployments, creating an expansive attack surface where traditional Security Operations Center (SOC) workflows struggle with long operational Mean Time to Respond (MTTR). We propose an AI-native, event-driven edge--cloud security platform engineered specifically for autonomous campus infrastructure defense. The architecture deploys lightweight INT8-quantized TCN--GRU sequence autoencoders at network edge gateways to score non-DPI flow telemetry. Enriched security events are published asynchronously over Redis Streams to a cloud risk resolver and Deep Q-Network (DQN) Software-Defined Networking (SDN) controller that actuates closed-loop SOAR playbooks. Evaluated on \textbf{real InSDN} flow windows~\cite{b1} ($20{,}000$ stratified samples), the platform achieves an end-to-end \emph{software control-path} latency --- telemetry ingestion to playbook emission, excluding dataplane enforcement --- of $5.909 \pm 2.495$~ms (95\% CI: $[5.755, 6.064]$~ms). Because stride-1 windows overlap by nine of ten timesteps, we evaluate under a temporally blocked and purged split with a train-only scaler, and quantifying that choice yields an unexpected null result: a conventional random split inflates fixed-threshold $F1$ only from $0.9276$ to $0.9337$, so window overlap is here a minor confound rather than the headline artefact we set out to document. Under the controlled protocol the INT8 detector reaches $F1 = 0.9276$ at $\tau = 0.65$ with a $0.048$~MB footprint and no measurable quantization loss ($\Delta F1 = 0.0000$). We further report three negative results: reconstruction-error scoring, the conventional rule for sequence autoencoders, is unusable on this attack-heavy corpus ($F1 = 0.036$), so detection rests entirely on the supervised head; our edge-resource hypothesis fails, delivering $8{,}171$ of $10{,}000$ offered windows/s at $43.8\%$ of one core against a pre-specified $10\%$ budget; and zero-shot transfer to CIC-IDS2017 collapses ($F1 = 0.461$). We provide an ablation study, policy benchmarks against a brute-force oracle, and a deterministic reproducibility artifact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is reshaping higher-education digital infrastructure, but university campus cybersecurity faces an increasingly intractable threat landscape: IoT sensors, student Bring-Your-Own-Device (BYOD) endpoints, open research laboratories, and administrative databases share one network fabric. Centralized SIEM and human-in-the-loop SOC pipelines exhibit long MTTR during volumetric DDoS, ransomware lateral movement, or data exfiltration~\cite{b4}, causing analyst burnout and delayed containment.

To bridge this gap we propose an \textbf{AI-Native Event-Driven Edge--Cloud Security Architecture}. Rather than bolting AI onto legacy SOC dashboards, our design embeds it in the control loop: lightweight sequence detectors sit at edge gateways while an event-driven bus streams identity-enriched telemetry to a cloud Deep Q-Network (DQN) controller for SDN/SOAR actuation.

\subsection{Research Questions}
This investigation is guided by four formal Research Questions (RQs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Control-Path Latency):} Can an event-driven edge--cloud architecture reduce the software decision path, from telemetry ingestion to containment playbook emission, to sub-10~ms levels?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Quantization \& Accuracy):} Does INT8 dynamic quantization maintain NIDS classification accuracy while satisfying an edge gateway \emph{model-footprint} constraint ($\le 1$~MB of model storage)?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Reinforcement Learning Efficiency):} Does a DQN policy controller outperform static rule tables and heuristic policies under modeled contention states?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Domain Shift Generalization):} How effectively does an in-domain trained edge model generalize zero-shot across disparate network datasets (InSDN $\rightarrow$ CIC-IDS2017)?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Formal Hypotheses}
We test three explicit hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Quantization Degradation):} INT8 dynamic quantization incurs less than $0.5\%$ $F1$-score degradation ($\Delta F1 \le 0.005$) compared to full-precision FP32 models.
    \item \textbf{H2 (Automated Decision Speedup):} Automated decision and playbook generation achieves a $>99.9\%$ reduction in time-to-decision relative to the adopted SOC baseline.
    \item \textbf{H3 (Edge Resource Feasibility):} Edge inference requires $\le 10\%$ CPU utilization per core under a sustained offered load of $10{,}000$~telemetry windows/sec.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}
The principal contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled AI-Native Pipeline:} An event-driven architecture combining PyTorch INT8 TCN--GRU edge detectors, identity context fusion, and Redis Streams pub/sub, with a DQN SDN controller driving OpenFlow playbooks (rate limiting, rerouting, isolation, token revocation).
    \item \textbf{Empirical Validation \& Negative Results:} Microbenchmarks on real InSDN~\cite{b1} traffic, with ablation, policy benchmarks against a brute-force oracle, saturation analysis, and a CIC-IDS2017~\cite{b2} holdout. We report four negative results --- failure of reconstruction-error scoring, a leakage effect far smaller than hypothesized, rejection of our edge resource hypothesis, and severe zero-shot transfer failure --- that bound where this design is deployable.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Artifact:} A reproducibility artifact regenerating every table from one seeded entry point, at \url{https://github.com/thtcsec/icai-fai2026}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
SDN intrusion datasets such as InSDN~\cite{b1} and CSE-CIC-IDS2018/2017~\cite{b2} have established standard benchmarks for network intrusion detection. Sequence models such as Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN)~\cite{b3} employ causal dilated convolutions to capture long-range temporal dependencies with parallelizable training compared to recurrent LSTM architectures. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has been applied to DDoS defense and 6G control loops~\cite{b4}; however, existing works predominantly terminate at offline detection or isolated controller simulations rather than end-to-end, sub-10~ms edge--cloud mitigation.

Compressing detectors for constrained gateways is an active line: pruned and quantized LSTM ensembles on Raspberry~Pi~\cite{b20} and INT8 TensorFlow Lite CNNs on ARM Cortex-M~\cite{b21} both find INT8 accuracy-neutral at far lower footprint, consistent with our H1, but both stop at the detector and close no loop onto actuation. Conversely, surveys of agent-driven security operations~\cite{b22} catalogue triage automation while noting that end-to-end autonomous remediation remains prospective; our work sits between the two. A parallel thread concerns evaluation rigour: Arp \emph{et al.}~\cite{b23} show data snooping is endemic in learning-based security papers and inflates results, and leakage-free reassessments of ML-NIDS confirm preprocessing alone moves headline metrics~\cite{b24}. We adopt those recommendations in Section~\ref{sec:protocol} and report their cost in Section~\ref{sec:leakage_effect}.

\section{AI-Native System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
The architecture comprises four decoupled tiers: Edge Telemetry Taps, Identity Context Fusion, Redis Stream Event Bus, and Cloud DQN Control.

\subsection{Edge Telemetry and TCN--GRU Detector}
Edge taps capture flow headers and extract 10-D flow feature vectors over sliding time windows ($\Delta t = 1.0$~s). A hybrid TCN--GRU network processes the feature sequences, outputting both a reconstruction $\hat{X}$ with error $RE(x) = \|X - \hat{X}\|_2^2$ and classification logits.

\textbf{Training objective.} The model is trained \emph{multi-task}, which determines how the results should be read. Each epoch runs a reconstruction pass minimizing $\mathrm{MSE}(X,\hat{X})$ over \emph{normal windows only}, then a joint pass minimizing $\mathrm{MSE}(X,\hat{X}) + \lambda\,\mathrm{CE}(\text{logits}, y)$ over all windows with $\lambda = 0.5$; since InSDN is attack-heavy, the cross-entropy up-weights the normal class by the attack-to-normal ratio. The detector is therefore \emph{not} unsupervised: it requires labels, and the autoencoder branch is an auxiliary representation objective, not the decision rule. Detection evaluates the supervised head, $P(\text{attack}) = 1 - P(\text{normal})$, against $\tau$; reconstruction error is not the operational score, for the reason in Section~\ref{sec:re_vs_cls}.

\subsection{Dynamic INT8 Quantization}
To satisfy memory-constrained edge gateways, PyTorch~\cite{b12} dynamic INT8 quantization compresses linear and GRU layer weight matrices from 32-bit floating point to 8-bit integers. We report no feature attribution study, since our artifact does not implement one and we prefer to leave the question open rather than assert an unmeasured importance ranking. Identifying which of the 10 features carry the discriminative signal, and whether that ranking is what fails to transfer (Section~\ref{sec:cross}), is future work.

\subsection{Identity Context Fusion \& Privacy Preservation}
Edge security events are enriched by querying local DHCP/RADIUS tables to append spatial-temporal context vectors:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E} = \{ \text{FlowID}, \text{RE}(x), P(\text{attack}), \text{UserRole}, \text{TrustScore} \}.
\end{equation}
To support GDPR data-minimization principles, raw payload inspection (DPI) is omitted entirely; only header-level statistical metadata is processed, and user identifiers are pseudonymized prior to cloud transport.

\subsection{Cloud Risk Engine and DQN SDN Control}
Enriched events stream asynchronously to Redis topic \texttt{security:telemetry:stream}. The cloud risk engine calculates a composite risk index $R$ over the detector's attack probability, the identity trust score, and asset criticality:
\begin{equation}
R = w_1 P(\text{attack}) + w_2 (1-\text{TrustScore}) + w_3 \text{AssetCriticality},
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
with $(w_1, w_2, w_3) = (0.60, 0.25, 0.15)$ in all reported experiments; the fused term is the classifier probability, not $RE(x)$. When identity is ablated the trust term is undefined and its weight folds into detection, giving $R = 0.85\,P(\text{attack}) + 0.15\,\text{AssetCriticality}$, keeping scores comparable across arms. The escalation ladder maps $R \ge 0.70$ to critical (block and revoke token), $R \ge 0.50$ to high (quarantine VLAN), and $R \ge 0.35$ to medium (throttle bandwidth).

Two caveats. The weights are hand-set, not learned, and encode a design prior we do not claim is optimal. More importantly, InSDN carries no asset inventory, so AssetCriticality is drawn i.i.d.\ uniform on $[0,1]$ in every experiment and is pure noise; the third term therefore \emph{degrades} our numbers, and the risk-engine benefit reported later comes from the trust term alone.

At the selected severity, the Deep Q-Network (DQN) policy agent~\cite{b5} evaluates state vector $s_t$ and selects containment action $a_t \in \{a_0: \text{Forward}, a_1: \text{Rate-Limit}, a_2: \text{Reroute}, a_3: \text{Isolate}\}$ to actuate automated SOAR playbooks~\cite{b18}. Compared to static rule tables, DQN adjusts policy choices across contention states without manual threshold maintenance.

\subsection{Dataset Sampling \& Leakage Control}
\label{sec:protocol}
We evaluate on the public \textbf{InSDN} dataset~\cite{b1}. Sliding-window aggregation of the released flow records ($\Delta t = 1.0$~s, sequence length $T=10$, \emph{stride 1}) yields $49{,}991$ windows, of which $40{,}043$ are attack and $9{,}948$ normal. Note that $49{,}991$ is the size of \emph{our} windowed corpus, not InSDN's native flow count.

Window $i$ shares $T-1 = 9$ of its $10$ timesteps with each neighbour, so an i.i.d.\ random split places near-duplicate observations on both sides of the train/test boundary --- precisely the sampling and snooping pitfalls catalogued in~\cite{b23,b24}. Our protocol instead (i) cuts the temporally ordered corpus into $20$ contiguous blocks and assigns whole blocks to train or test; (ii) \emph{purges} $9$ windows from both ends of every block, guaranteeing no surviving training window shares even a single timestep with a test window; (iii) stratified-subsamples each side independently to $14{,}000$ train and $6{,}000$ test windows, preserving class balance; and (iv) fits the scaler on the training partition only, transforming the test partition and the CIC-IDS2017 holdout with those frozen statistics. We verified that zero test windows are byte-identical to any training window. Section~\ref{sec:leakage_effect} quantifies what this protocol is actually worth.

\subsection{Detection Performance \& Threshold Sensitivity (RQ2, H1)}
\label{sec:threshold}
Table~\ref{tab:precision} shows metric variation across decision threshold $\tau$ under the leakage-controlled protocol. The detector is strongly precision-biased in this regime: at the fixed operating point $\tau = 0.65$ it yields $F1 = 0.9276$ (Precision~$=0.979$, Recall~$=0.882$, FPR~$=7.78\%$), and over the reported grid $F1$ peaks at $0.950$ at $\tau = 0.35$. \subsection{Why Reconstruction Error Is Not the Decision Rule}
\label{sec:re_vs_cls}
Since much of the sequence-autoencoder IDS literature scores on reconstruction error alone, it is worth reporting which of our two outputs actually works. We evaluated the standard unsupervised rule $\tau = \mu + k\sigma$, with $\mu, \sigma$ estimated on reconstruction errors of \emph{normal training} windows, for $k \in \{0.5, \ldots, 3.0\}$. It fails comprehensively: the best setting $k=0.5$ yields $F1 = 0.0358$ (Precision~$=0.752$, Recall~$=0.018$), decaying to $0.0062$ at $k=3.0$. Precision reaches $0.882$ at $k=2.5$, but at recall below $2\%$, which is worthless operationally.

Two conclusions follow. Detection performance is attributable to the supervised head, so the architecture should be cited as a supervised sequence classifier with an auxiliary reconstruction objective. And on an attack-heavy corpus the premise of reconstruction-based detection is violated: the model sees enough attack structure in the joint pass that attacks reconstruct about as well as normal traffic. Practitioners inheriting the ``train on normal traffic, threshold the error'' recipe should check it against their own class balance.

\subsection{Throughput Saturation \& Edge Feasibility (H3)}
Per-inference latency and throughput are only reconcilable once the batching regime is stated, so we report the two separately. Table~\ref{tab:saturation} gives saturation throughput --- the detector run flat out --- against inference batch size. Unbatched scoring (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) saturates at $157$~windows/s, nearly two orders of magnitude short. Batching helps but not enough: batch $=128$ saturates at $7{,}714$~windows/s, and only batch $=512$ clears target ($20{,}087$~windows/s) at a $25.5$~ms per-batch forward pass. Table~\ref{tab:resource} reports a \emph{paced} offered load at batch $=128$, rate-limited so a shortfall surfaces rather than hiding. The three lower targets are met within $0.4\%$; $10{,}000$~windows/s is \emph{not}, delivering $8{,}171$.

\textbf{H3 is rejected on both terms.} The hypothesis asserted $\le 10\%$ CPU per core \emph{at} a sustained $10{,}000$~windows/s; the system neither sustains that load at batch $=128$ nor stays in budget at the $8{,}171$~windows/s it does achieve, consuming $43.8\%$ of a core. The budget holds only at $100$~windows/s ($6.1\%$); the jump to $39.1\%$ at $1{,}000$~windows/s then near-flat scaling indicates a fixed per-batch dispatch cost dominating until the pipeline is busy. We report this rather than relaxing the threshold or quietly switching to the batch $=512$ configuration that would have met target. Memory is favourable --- RSS is flat at $495$~MB across a $100\times$ load range --- so the system is memory- but not CPU-feasible on one commodity core. A compiled ONNX or TensorRT runtime is the obvious next step, making this an implementation rather than architectural limit.

We deliberately omit a legacy inline-DPI comparison. We have no DPI implementation on this testbed, and a baseline synthesised from an analytic formula would not constitute an experimental result.

\subsection{SOTA Model Comparison \& Quantization Degradation (H1)}
\label{sec:sota}
Table~\ref{tab:sota} compares our INT8 TCN--GRU against baselines trained on the identical InSDN split; Transformer AE is a 2-layer encoder-decoder with 4 attention heads and masked reconstruction loss. Every model is scored at its own optimal threshold, since a threshold favouring one architecture need not suit another. \emph{That threshold is selected by searching the test split}, so each $F1$ here is an optimistic bound, not a deployable operating point; the identical search is applied to every model, so the comparison between rows stays internally fair. Deployable numbers are those at the fixed $\tau$ of Table~\ref{tab:precision}. TCN--GRU attains $F1 = 0.9725$ against $0.8680$ for Transformer AE and $0.8687$ for LSTM, a margin of roughly $10$ points, from a single seeded run; we claim no significance across seeds.

Two caveats. The Isolation Forest row uses \texttt{contamination="auto"} on time-averaged windows; on an $80\%$-attack corpus this heuristic is badly mismatched, so $F1 = 0.2000$ evidences that an untuned classical outlier detector fails at this class balance rather than serving as a tuned baseline. We also omit a per-model CPU column: the only CPU figures we measured are those of the deployed detector under paced load (Table~\ref{tab:resource}), and we prefer an absent column to numbers we cannot substantiate.

INT8 quantization reduces the model footprint from $0.104$~MB to $0.048$~MB ($53.8\%$) at \emph{zero} measured accuracy cost ($\Delta F1 = 0.0000$ to four decimal places), supporting hypothesis \textbf{H1}. Counter-intuitively, dynamic INT8 on x86 CPU \emph{increases} latency ($11.117$~ms vs $6.554$~ms, a $1.70\times$ penalty) because activations are dequantized at every layer boundary without hardware INT8 acceleration: the technique buys memory, not speed, here. Since the RQ2 budget was $1$~MB and the FP32 model already meets it at $0.104$~MB, the honest reading of H1 is that quantization is \emph{harmless} rather than necessary, and justified only for gateways an order of magnitude more constrained than ours.

\subsection{Component-Wise Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}

Because each configuration scores on a different scale, we select the threshold on the training split and apply it unchanged to test; a shared fixed threshold would measure calibration mismatch instead of component contribution. InSDN has no identity annotations, so trust comes from a simulated overlay parameterised by $\rho = P(\text{low trust} \mid \text{attack})$, reported at $\rho = 0.7$.

The result contradicts our expectation. Identity fusion and the risk engine \emph{do not} improve $F1$; removing them nudges it from $0.9602$ up to $0.9680$. Since the controller autonomously isolates flows, trading $0.8$ points of $F1$ for less than half the false alarms is operationally correct --- but it is a trade, not a free gain. A sweep over $\rho \in [0.5, 0.9]$ (in the artifact) shows identity fusion improves $F1$ only once the overlay is strongly informative ($\rho \ge 0.8$), bounding what this mechanism can claim without real campus identity data.

\subsection{Policy Control Benchmarks (RQ3)}
Table~\ref{tab:policy_benchmark} benchmarks the cloud DQN controller against static and heuristic baselines on a single-step contention environment whose reward $r(s,a)$ trades containment efficacy against collateral QoS damage and congestion relief. Because $r$ is analytic, the optimal action $a^*$ is obtained by exact enumeration over the four actions, giving a ground-truth oracle. The agent is trained by fitted Q-iteration over $4{,}000$ environment interactions with $\epsilon$-greedy exploration and evaluated on $5{,}000$ held-out states.

The DQN selects the oracle-optimal action in $99.2\%$ of states and attains essentially zero mean regret, against $38.2\%$ for a static confidence ladder and $34.8\%$ for a hand-tuned heuristic table. Two caveats temper this. First, the agent is trained on the same reward function the oracle maximises, so this shows function-approximation capacity within a known environment, not generalisation to a live campus. Second, counter to expectation, the DQN inflicts \emph{more} raw QoS disruption on benign traffic ($0.128$) than the static ladder ($0.045$): reward-optimal behaviour is more aggressive than a conservative threshold rule, and the learned policy's advantage lies in near-zero regret rather than gentleness.

\subsection{How Much Did Leakage Inflate the Numbers?}
\label{sec:leakage_effect}
Rather than assert our leakage controls were necessary, we measured what they are worth, rerunning the identical detector, seed, and schedule while changing only the split and scaler. Protocol~A is the unsound one (i.i.d.\ split over overlapping windows, scaler fit on the pooled corpus); Protocol~B is that of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}.

\textbf{The effect is real but small, and this is a negative result.} Leakage inflates fixed-threshold $F1$ by $0.0061$ ($0.9276 \rightarrow 0.9337$) and optimal-threshold $F1$ by $0.0021$. We expected a far larger gap, since $9$ of every $10$ timesteps cross the boundary, and report the modest magnitude because the opposite claim would have been more publishable.

Two explanations fit and we cannot separate them: overlapping windows may be less informative than they look, since the one differing timestep is precisely the one carrying the transition; or this corpus may be easy enough that both protocols saturate above $0.92$. The defensible guidance is therefore narrower than we hoped --- blocked-and-purged splitting is cheap and principled and we recommend it, but a reviewer should not assume a random split has by itself inflated a result by a large margin.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Domain Shift Evaluation (RQ4)}
\label{sec:cross}
Table~\ref{tab:cross} evaluates zero-shot transfer from InSDN ($14{,}000$ train / $6{,}000$ test windows) to a $20{,}000$-window CIC-IDS2017 holdout. In-domain $F1$ is $0.9276$ at $\tau=0.65$, whereas zero-shot $F1$ collapses to $0.4611$, demonstrating severe cross-dataset domain shift. The failure mode is precision, not recall: the detector still recovers $92.8\%$ of attacks but its false positive rate explodes to $92.4\%$, close to a degenerate always-attack classifier. Since detection is driven by the supervised head (Section~\ref{sec:re_vs_cls}), the reading is that the \emph{decision boundary} does not transfer: CIC-IDS2017 benign traffic falls on the attack side of a boundary fitted to InSDN statistics. Re-tuning to $\tau^*=0.0720$ does not help ($F1 = 0.4670$), so the degradation is representational. A contributing factor we did not isolate is that the corpora were feature-extracted under different tooling and flow-timeout conventions, so the $10$ features may not be semantically identical.

\section{Threats to Validity \& Limitations}
\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:validity}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Internal Validity:} Timings are subject to CPU scheduling noise; we averaged $1{,}000$ runs with nanosecond timers, yet repeating the suite under different background load produced end-to-end means from $1.9$ to $7.9$~ms. We report a single complete run so every latency here is mutually consistent, and treat absolute latencies as order-of-magnitude estimates. The sub-10~ms RQ1 result holds on our testbed with under a factor of two of headroom and is not a hardware-independent guarantee.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility:} An earlier version of this artifact constructed models before seeding the global RNG, so detection metrics varied between runs of the same script. We fixed this (\texttt{set\_global\_seed} now precedes every module instantiation) and regenerated all numbers from one deterministic execution. We flag it because the failure mode is silent and common: seeding inside a training helper is too late if the module already exists.
    \item \textbf{External Validity:} Evaluation uses public InSDN and CIC benchmarks; broader IoT/IIoT corpora such as UNSW-NB15~\cite{b6}, ToN\_IoT~\cite{b7}, and Edge-IIoTset~\cite{b8} offer complementary vectors for multi-campus validation.
    \item \textbf{Construct Validity:} Metrics assume clean flow labels; noisy headers could affect precision. The SOC and SIEM rows of Table~\ref{tab:mttr} are stipulated, establishing an order of magnitude and nothing finer.
    \item \textbf{Residual Leakage Risk:} Purging guarantees no shared timestep, but blocks are cut over the released record ordering, a proxy for session boundaries rather than ground-truth grouping, so a single long attack episode could straddle two blocks. Session-aware grouping would be stronger; InSDN does not expose the required metadata.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations \& Future Work}
Primary limitations: (i) a single-controller emulated SDN testbed rather than production hardware; (ii) no evaluation of adversarial robustness against evasion, the obvious attack on any AI-native control loop; (iii) severe zero-shot cross-dataset degradation; (iv) rejection of H3, as the Python runtime neither sustains $10{,}000$~windows/s at batch $=128$ nor stays within budget at the load it does sustain; (v) an operating point that misses roughly one attack window in eight at $\tau=0.65$, or trades that for a $12.1\%$ full-pipeline false positive rate when tuned --- either a material cost when the controller autonomously isolates flows; and (vi) single-seed reporting, so run-to-run variance is unquantified even though our reproducibility investigation showed it exists. Future work: domain adaptation, compiled ONNX/TensorRT edge runtimes with batched inference, online threshold tuning, multi-seed reporting, and federated multi-campus learning.

\section{Ethics, Privacy \& Public Artifact}
\subsection{Privacy Posture}
The architecture operates strictly on non-DPI flow headers; payloads are uninspected and IP/MAC identifiers are \emph{pseudonymized} via SHA-256 before transport, supporting data minimization under GDPR and Vietnam's Decree 13/2023. We avoid ``anonymized'': an unkeyed SHA-256 over a $32$-bit IPv4 or $48$-bit MAC address is trivially invertible by enumeration, so the hashes resist casual inspection but not a motivated adversary. A keyed HMAC with a rotated per-deployment secret is the defensible construction; the unkeyed hash is a prototype placeholder, not a privacy guarantee.

\subsection{Public Research Artifact}
Code, dataset preprocessors, model checkpoints, Docker orchestration files, and execution scripts are available under MIT License at \url{https://github.com/thtcsec/icai-fai2026}. Every table here is regenerated by one entry point, \texttt{results/run\_all\_and\_summarize.py}, which seeds all generators (seed \texttt{42}) before model construction and emits the machine-readable summary the tables are transcribed from.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented an AI-native event-driven edge--cloud architecture for autonomous campus network security. Combining INT8-quantized TCN--GRU edge detectors, Redis Streams transport, and cloud DQN SDN actuation, it achieves a sub-10~ms software control path ($5.909 \pm 2.495$~ms) from telemetry ingestion to playbook emission. We stop short of calling this closed-loop mitigation: dataplane enforcement is unmeasured and the SOC comparison uses stipulated baselines.

The results we think transfer beyond this system are the negative ones. Reconstruction-error scoring, the default rule for sequence autoencoders, is unusable on an attack-heavy corpus ($F1 = 0.036$), so what we report is a supervised classifier with an auxiliary reconstruction objective and should be cited as such. Leakage from stride-1 overlap is real but, contrary to expectation, small ($\Delta F1 = 0.006$), so we cannot support the stronger cautionary claim we set out to make. H3 fails on both terms, delivering $8{,}171$ of $10{,}000$ offered windows/s at $43.8\%$ of one core against a $10\%$ budget. Zero-shot transfer to CIC-IDS2017 collapses to $F1 = 0.46$, driven by precision, locating the problem in a decision boundary that does not survive a change of capture domain. H1 alone is cleanly supported --- INT8 is accuracy-neutral at half the footprint --- though where the FP32 model already fits, that buys little. Together these bound where an AI-native campus defense is currently practical.
